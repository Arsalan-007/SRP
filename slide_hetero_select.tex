\begin{frame}{Question 4: Do Diverse Experts, Chosen on Validation, Beat Averaging?}
    \small
    \textbf{Test:} 6 benchmarks, 5 seeds, trained to convergence. Every expert draws its own
    depth, dropout, learning rate, weight decay and PLR $\sigma$ at random; each trains
    independently; greedy selection\,{\scriptsize[Caruana 2004]} rebuilds the ensemble each
    epoch from \textbf{validation} predictions.
    \vspace{0.1cm}
    \begin{table}
        \centering
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}
        \footnotesize
        \begin{tabular}{l | c | c | l}
            \rowcolor{PrimaryColor} \color{white}\textbf{Dataset} & \color{white}\textbf{Averaging} & \color{white}\textbf{Diverse + selection} & \color{white}\textbf{Better} \\
            California Housing (RMSE$\downarrow$) & 0.4796 & \textbf{0.4678} & tie ($p=0.26$) \\
            \rowcolor{LightGrey} Adult (accuracy$\uparrow$) & 0.8594 & \textbf{0.8675} & selection ($p=0.002$) \\
            Jannis (accuracy$\uparrow$) & 0.7194 & \textbf{0.7224} & selection ($p=0.019$) \\
            \rowcolor{LightGrey} Higgs Small (accuracy$\uparrow$) & \textbf{0.7250} & 0.7199 & averaging ($p=0.041$) \\
            YearPredictionMSD (RMSE$\downarrow$) & \textbf{8.8247} & 8.8268 & tie ($p=0.90$) \\
            \rowcolor{LightGrey} Covertype (accuracy$\uparrow$) & \textbf{0.9488} & 0.8714 & averaging ($p<0.001$) \\
        \end{tabular}
    \end{table}
    \vspace{-0.1cm}
    \textbf{Result:} 2 wins, 2 losses, 2 ties. Pooled over all 30 pairs, \textbf{Wilcoxon $p=0.58$}
    --- \textbf{no general improvement}, and the pre-registered rule returns \emph{size-dependent}.
    Cost: $2.9\times$ the parameters, $3.6\times$ the training time.\\[0.1cm]
    \tiny Averaging here is the current v4 (mean-pool + PLR, 5 seeds, trained to convergence), so it is
    stronger than the 60-epoch, no-PLR baseline used in the earlier attention comparison.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What Actually Worked: Selecting on Validation, Not Learning Weights}
    \small
    Earlier experiments tried attention, softmax, sigmoid and Gumbel weighting. All learned their
    weights on \textbf{training} data, jointly with the experts, and all failed. Greedy selection
    differs in one respect: it uses \textbf{held-out validation} data and only decides
    \emph{include} or \emph{exclude}.
    \vspace{0.15cm}
    \begin{table}
        \centering
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}
        \footnotesize
        \begin{tabular}{l | c | c | c | c}
            \rowcolor{PrimaryColor} \color{white}\textbf{Dataset} & \color{white}\textbf{Average all 16} & \color{white}\textbf{Selected} & \color{white}\textbf{Gain} & \color{white}\textbf{Experts kept} \\
            California Housing (RMSE$\downarrow$) & 0.5196 & \textbf{0.4678} & $+9.97\%$ & 6.6 / 16 \\
            \rowcolor{LightGrey} Adult (accuracy$\uparrow$) & 0.8652 & \textbf{0.8675} & $+0.27\%$ & 5.0 / 16 \\
            Jannis (accuracy$\uparrow$) & 0.7074 & \textbf{0.7224} & $+2.13\%$ & 6.8 / 16 \\
            \rowcolor{LightGrey} Higgs Small (accuracy$\uparrow$) & 0.7122 & \textbf{0.7199} & $+1.07\%$ & 5.8 / 16 \\
            YearPredictionMSD (RMSE$\downarrow$) & 8.9444 & \textbf{8.8268} & $+1.31\%$ & 7.2 / 16 \\
            \rowcolor{LightGrey} Covertype (accuracy$\uparrow$) & 0.8414 & \textbf{0.8714} & $+3.57\%$ & 6.0 / 16 \\
        \end{tabular}
    \end{table}
    \vspace{-0.1cm}
    \begin{block}{Finding}
        Selection wins on \textbf{6 of 6} datasets and \textbf{30 of 30} individual runs
        (pooled Wilcoxon $p<0.0001$), and it discards roughly \textbf{two thirds} of the experts.
        Held-out selection succeeds exactly where training-time weighting failed.
    \end{block}
\end{frame}
